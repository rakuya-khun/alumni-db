Chapter 1 In contemporary higher education, institutions are expected to demonstrate the effectiveness of their academic programs through measurable graduate outcomes. This mandate aligns with Commission on Higher Education (CHED) Memorandum Order No. 46, Series of 2012, which institutionalized Outcomes-Based Education (OBE) across Philippine higher education institutions (CHED, 2012). Under this framework, universities must articulate intended learning outcomes and provide evidence that these outcomes are achieved beyond graduation. Global perspectives further highlight the importance of graduate tracking systems. The World Bank (2022) identifies employability as a key indicator of higher education quality , particularly in developing economies, while the Asian Development Bank (2021) emphasizes digital data systems as essential tools for strengthening governance and evidence-based decision-making. Moreover , accreditation frameworks aligned with the Washington Accord stress documented graduate attributes and continuous improvement processes (International Engineering Alliance [IEA], 2021). As the Philippine Technological Council (PTC), through ACBET , adopts these standards, institutions must present verifiable evidence of graduate achievement and professional readiness (Philippine Technological Council, 2023). For the College of Engineering at Southern Luzon State University (SLSU), which aims to pursue PTC–ACBET accreditation by 2027, the development of a structured and optimized alumni database system is therefore a strategic priority . Such a system will enable systematic tracking of employment outcomes, support accreditation requirements, and strengthen outcomes-based evaluation and institutional planning.

Backgr ound and Definition of the Project The pursuit of accreditation from the Philippine Technological Council (PTC) and the Accreditation and Certification Board for Engineering and Technology (ACBET) represents a strategic imperative for engineering institutions in the Philippines seeking to demonstrate educational quality and global competitiveness. As Southern Luzon State University (SLSU) aims to achieve PTC-ACBET accreditation by 2027, the institution must demonstrate compliance with rigorous outcome-based education (OBE) standards that require systematic evidence of graduate performance and professional development. According to the PTC, accreditation serves as a mechanism to ensure that engineering programs produce graduates who meet both national professional standards and international benchmarks, thereby enhancing the global competitiveness and employability of engineering graduates (Philippine Technological Council, 2023). This accreditation framework, which aligns with the Washington Accord to which ACBET holds provisional signatory status, necessitates that institutions implement robust data collection systems to track and evaluate alumni outcomes as evidence of program effectiveness (International Engineering Alliance, 2023). The ability to systematically demonstrate graduate success through empirical data has become not merely desirable but essential for meeting the documentation requirements mandated by accrediting bodies. D. System Usability Evaluation A critical component of Objective 4 is the evaluation of the system's usability and acceptability using the System Usability Scale (SUS). The following literature provides the theoretical and methodological basis for this evaluation approach. Bangor , Kortum, and Miller (2008) conducted a large-scale empirical study validating the SUS across over 2,300 surveys spanning ten years, establishing adjective-based interpretations: scores between 85–100 correspond to "Excellent," 73–85 to "Good," 52–73 to "OK," and below 52 to "Poor ." This adjective scale provides a more intuitive interpretation for non-specialist evaluators such as university administrators, making it appropriate for the system evaluation in this study . Lewis (2018) further validated the SUS as a reliable and valid instrument for measuring perceived usability , noting that it demonstrates high internal consistency (Cronbach's alpha typically exceeding 0.90) and that its two-factor structure — capturing both usability and learnability — is well-suited for evaluating information systems in educational contexts where end-users may have varying levels of technical proficiency . Chapter III METHODOLOGY This study adopts a systems-oriented and Industrial Engineering (IE)–based methodology to design, develop, and optimize an alumni database system for outcome-based evaluation and institutional networking in the College of Engineering at Southern Luzon State University , specifically Civil, Computer and Electrical Engineering alumni. The methodology is divided into two major phases: (1) Systems Analysis and Design and (2) System Implementation and Evaluation. Each phase explicitly applies IE tools to ensure efficiency , effectiveness, and decision-support capability . Project Design This study employs a developmental and descriptive research design grounded in Industrial Engineering (IE) systems analysis and design principles. The developmental aspect focuses on the design, optimization, and implementation of an alumni database system for outcome-based evaluation, while the descriptive component examines the existing alumni data management processes and information gaps within Southern Luzon State University . The research is conducted in two phases. Phase 1: Systems Analysis and Design applies IE tools such as needs assessment, stakeholder analysis, process mapping, data flow diagramming, and entity–relationship modeling to analyze the system requirements and the current alumni data processes.

=== PAGE 33 ===
Phase 2: System Implementation and Evaluation involves the development of the offline alumni database system with spreadsheet synchronization functionality , followed by system testing, data validation, performance evaluation, and process optimization using IE principles such as lean process improvement and key performance indicators (KPIs). The outcome of the research is a functional, cost-ef fective, and decision-support-oriented alumni database system that supports outcome-based evaluation and institutional networking within the College of Engineering. Project Setting The study is conducted at Southern Luzon State University (SLSU), specifically within the College of Engineering, which offers Civil Engineering, Computer Engineering, and Electrical Engineering programs. The College serves as the primary setting and beneficiary of the proposed alumni database system. Key institutional stakeholders involved in the study include the Dean of the College of Engineering, college chairpersons, and designated system administrators, who will act as authorized users of the system. Additional contextual inputs are derived from existing alumni data practices of the Office of Student Affairs and Services (OSAS), the SLSU Alumni Federation, Inc., and the SLSU Engineering Alumni League (SEAL). Alumni data utilized in the study pertain to engineering graduates, particularly those who graduated in 2018, representing the target group for five-year post-graduation outcome-based evaluation. The proposed system will be hosted locally within the College of Engineering and will not be directly accessible to alumni, ensuring administrative control, data privacy , and institutional ownership of alumni performance data.
